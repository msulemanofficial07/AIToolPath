Best AI for Research and Recent Information

For recent facts, the best workflow is not simply “ask an AI.” I separate finding sources, analyzing supplied material, and checking claims—and choose tools for each step.

Research is more than a generated answer

I use AI to reduce friction at the start: break down a question, discover useful terms, or organize material I already found. For a current claim, the core work remains finding evidence, checking dates, and judging the source. A fluent summary is not a bibliography.

Tool roles I compare

  • Perplexity: search-first exploration and citations to inspect.
  • Google Gemini: consider current search, multimodal context, or eligible Google workflows.
  • Claude or ChatGPT: discuss and synthesize supplied material; verify outside facts and citations.
  • Grok: explore online conversation where enabled; social posts are leads, not evidence.

A source-check checklist

  1. Open the original source, not just the assistant's summary.
  2. Check author, date, method, context, and whether it supports the claim.
  3. Seek another authoritative source for important points.
  4. Record what you read and distinguish evidence from interpretation.
  5. Do not submit invented references or confidential research material.

For a dense PDF, ask a supported assistant to identify relevant pages, then inspect them yourself. It may miss a chart, footnote, qualification, or table. Verify current tools at Perplexity ↗, Gemini ↗, and Claude ↗.

My short summary

Start with a search-oriented tool for source discovery, use an assistant to analyze material, and verify important statements at their original source. Traceability matters more than answer polish.
