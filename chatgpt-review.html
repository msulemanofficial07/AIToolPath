My experience using ChatGPT since 2022

I am a freelancer, student, and researcher, and I have used ChatGPT for around four years. What keeps it in my toolkit is its range: I can move from a coding question to a study explanation, research outline, or client-work draft without changing tools. It is my flexible starting point, not an expert that I trust blindly.

How I use it for coding

I use ChatGPT to understand error messages, talk through an approach, explain unfamiliar code, and sketch a first solution. It is most helpful when I share the relevant context and ask it to explain its reasoning. I still run the code, check the changes, and test edge cases before using anything in a real project.

How it helps with study and research

For learning, I ask it to explain a difficult idea in steps, quiz me, or point out gaps in my reasoning. For research, it can help me narrow a broad topic into useful questions and search terms. I verify important facts against course materials and original sources; a confident answer or citation is not proof on its own.

Freelance work: a useful first draft

I use it to organize a brief, outline a proposal, polish an email, or get unstuck when starting a piece of work. I treat the result as a draft that I need to personalize and fact-check. I do not paste private client details, credentials, or sensitive student information unless the sharing is permitted and the account's data controls suit the situation.

Free plan, ChatGPT Plus, and model choice

ChatGPT has a free plan with limits. In the US, ChatGPT Plus is commonly listed at about $20 per month; the exact price, tax, features, and usage limits can vary by country and change, so I would check the official ChatGPT plans and pricing before subscribing. I choose the strongest model currently available in my own model selector for a demanding task rather than assuming an API model is included in every plan. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 documentation describes it for complex professional work, but I would use it in ChatGPT only if it appears in my account's model picker. API usage is billed separately from a ChatGPT subscription.

What I like: it handles a wide mix of coding, study, research planning, and freelance writing. What I watch out for: facts can be wrong, sources need checking, and generated code still needs testing.

My short summary

After around four years of use, ChatGPT remains my all-round starting point for coding, education, research planning, and freelance tasks. I would begin with the free plan and consider Plus only if the current features and limits are worth the local price for my workload.
