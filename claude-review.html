My Claude Review for Coding and Complex Documents

Why Claude stands out in my workflow

Across my work as a freelancer and student, I find Claude useful when I need to stay with a complex task rather than get a quick one-line answer. I use it to analyze substantial text, reorganize a dense brief, critique a draft, and talk through programming decisions. For coding, that can mean explaining an error or planning edits across files. I inspect every proposed change and run the project's checks.

Complex files and PDFs

Claude can help summarize supported documents, compare sections, extract questions, or point me to a passage to inspect. I treat its response as a map through a document, not a substitute for reading the relevant page. For tables, diagrams, footnotes, and academic or legal claims, I confirm exact wording in the source. Supported formats, size limits, and visual handling can change; check the Anthropic Help Center ↗.

Model choice and trade-offs

Anthropic's official model overview ↗ lists its current model family and links to model-selection guidance. Product and API model lists are not always identical. Compare the strongest model in your own account with a faster option on the same task, and verify current access before paying.

Claude can misread a requirement, invent a detail, or produce code that fails in the project. Long context is not perfect recall. Avoid uploading client or coursework material unless sharing it is permitted and the current data controls are acceptable.

My short summary

Claude is my pick to try for complex text, long-document analysis, and thoughtful coding discussions. Its output still needs source checking and tests; do not choose it solely because an article calls one model the latest.
