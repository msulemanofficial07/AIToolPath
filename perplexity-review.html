My experience using Perplexity for research

As a researcher, student, and freelancer, I often need a quick way to get oriented on a subject. Perplexity gives me a conversational starting point for finding pages, learning unfamiliar terms, and working out what I should read next. I use it to start research, not to finish it for me.

Recent information and source checking

When I need current information, the linked sources are useful leads. I open them and check the date, the author, and whether the page supports the exact claim in the answer. If the question matters, I look for an original study, official notice, or other primary source. A citation can point to a relevant page without proving every sentence in a summary.

How I use it for study and freelance work

For study, I use it to collect initial reading ideas and then work through the original material. For a freelance brief, it can help me identify background topics and questions to investigate before I write. I do not use an AI summary as a substitute for research, and I follow academic rules and client requirements.

Free plan, Perplexity Pro, and price

Perplexity offers free access with limits. Perplexity Pro is commonly listed at about $20 per month in the US; local prices, taxes, billing periods, and plan features can vary. I would check Perplexity's official Pro page for the current offer in my region before subscribing. Try free access first and upgrade only if the extra research capacity is useful for your routine.

What I like: a search-first workflow and visible links that help me follow a topic. What I watch out for: sources can be weak or misrepresented, and every important citation needs to be opened and checked.

My short summary

Perplexity is the tool I would try first when I need to explore a recent web question and find sources to read. It helps me begin research, but the original sources—not the AI summary—are what I rely on.
