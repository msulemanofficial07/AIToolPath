My Perplexity Review for Current Research

What I use Perplexity for

My student and freelance work often starts with a question that needs context. Perplexity can make the first search-and-summarize step feel more conversational than a list of links. I use it to discover terminology, locate relevant pages, and identify what to read next before writing a brief or study plan.

How I check citations

A citation is a research lead, not an automatic fact-check. I open the source and ask: Is it original or a secondary summary? Does it support this exact sentence? Is it current enough? Does another reliable source agree? I also distinguish marketing pages, forum posts, studies, and official records; they serve different purposes.

For consequential health, legal, financial, safety, or academic claims, use authoritative sources and do not rely on a generated summary. A correct link can still be attached to an unsupported claim.

Strengths, limits, and who should try it

I would test Perplexity first for a question where web discovery and a visible trail to sources matter. Compare it with Gemini or a conventional search engine for a broad web query, and with Claude or ChatGPT for detailed analysis of material you already have. Students, writers, and freelancers who regularly need current sources may like a search-first workflow.

Features and plans change; check Perplexity's current product ↗. For dense PDFs, confirm account file limits and inspect cited pages yourself.

My short summary

Perplexity is my first stop for exploring a current web question and collecting source leads. I do not treat its summary or citations as verified until I read the original pages.
