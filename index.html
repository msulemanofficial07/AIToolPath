Editorial method

Evidence before verdicts.

Best-of lists age quickly. Any future review should name its task, selection criteria, evidence, and checked date instead of treating a universal ranking as permanent.

Current catalog status: 17 demonstration entries. None have been independently tested, and pricing and feature details are unverified. No ratings or “best” awards are assigned.
01Compare like with like. Test comparable tools against the same task and explain the user and workflow they suit.
02Check primary sources. Confirm changing prices, limits, features, platforms, and terms with the provider; show when checked.
03Write the trade-offs. Include setup, privacy, rights, failure modes, and who should consider an alternative.
04Keep commerce separate. Label affiliate and sponsored links; commercial relationships do not determine editorial fit.

CHOOSING A TOOL

Begin with the job, not the brand

A simple checklist for narrowing your options.

Read the note

Write down the task, the output you need, your budget ceiling, and any must-have privacy or platform requirements. Compare two or three tools against that list before signing up.

PRICING

Check the plan before you commit

Free tiers and trials can have important limits.

Read the note

Confirm current pricing, usage limits, renewal terms, and commercial-use rights on the provider's own site. Treat directory pricing as a snapshot with a verification date, not a promise.

WORKFLOW

Keep a person in the loop

Use AI as a tool, not an unquestioned authority.

Read the note

Review factual claims, permissions, and sensitive information before sharing or publishing AI-assisted work. Keep the workflow proportionate to the risk of the task.
